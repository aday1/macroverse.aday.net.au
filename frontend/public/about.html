The Fringe piece

Melbourne Fringe Abbotsford Convent Clan Analogue

MacroVerse began as a live sonic and visual work: an interpretation of the evolution of the universe, from energy stasis through heat-death dissipation. Electronic artist Reductionist (Nick Wilson) performed with battery-powered micro-instruments and micro-virtuosic technique, giving an embodied human focus in the room. Aday (Adrian Richardson) provided live video projection and improvisation that distorted time and energy flow, extending the body as the audience tried to grasp the infinite.

Presented as part of Abbotsford Convent’s experimental art program during Melbourne Fringe — MacroVerse — we had an hour-long performance session for a week.

From stage to software

This browser stack (macroverse.aday.net.au) carries forward that live-visual practice: GLSL/ISF shader browsing, VJ crossfades, parameter exposure, WebXR audience and controller modes, participatory gig sessions, and show-time routing. The Fringe piece is the testament; the app is the instrument built after it.
