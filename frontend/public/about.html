About MacroVerse

Origin story // live show to browser shader engine

Melbourne Fringe Abbotsford Convent Clan Analogue

MacroVerse began as a live sonic and visual work: an interpretation of the evolution of the universe, from energy stasis through heat-death dissipation. Electronic artist Reductionist (Nick Wilson) performed with battery-powered micro-instruments and micro-virtuosic technique, giving an embodied human focus in the room. Aday (Adrian Richardson) provided live video projection and improvisation that distorted time and energy flow, extending the body as the audience tried to grasp the infinite.

The work was presented as part of Abbotsford Convent’s experimental art program during Melbourne Fringe — MacroVerse. The exhibition ran free daily (12pm–4pm); ticketed performance sessions ran from 7pm each night for about one hour, alongside related contemporary works on the same program.

From stage to software

This browser stack (macroverse.aday.net.au) carries forward that live-visual practice: GLSL/ISF shader browsing, VJ crossfades, parameter exposure, and show-time routing. The Fringe piece is the testament; the app is the instrument built after it.
